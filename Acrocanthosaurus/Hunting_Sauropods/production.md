# Prehistoric Texas — Acrocanthosaurus Hunting Video Workflow

**Project:** Prehistoric Texas  
**Sequence:** Acrocanthosaurus stalking and spotting sauropod prey  
**Target Length:** 10–15 seconds  
**Working Target:** 12 seconds  
**Status:** Draft v1  
**Last Updated:** 2026-09-23

---

## 1) Revised Production Workflow

This workflow replaces a loosely structured generation process with a **living markdown production file** that ChatGPT can continuously update as the sequence evolves.

### Standard Pipeline
1. **ChatGPT develops the script and visual scene plan.**
2. **ChatGPT continuously updates this markdown file** with:
   - scene list
   - shot purposes
   - continuity rules
   - GPT Image start-frame prompts
   - GPT Image end-frame prompts
   - Kling motion prompts
   - revision notes
3. **GPT Image generates the start and end frames** for each shot.
4. **Kling Canvas organizes the sequence flow** and maps the relationships between shots, frames, and assets.
5. **Kling 3.0 generates each shot independently** using the defined start and end frames.
6. **ChatGPT revises this markdown file after tests** so the document remains the current production guide.

### Why this workflow is better
- Keeps the production process organized.
- Makes continuity easier to maintain across scenes.
- Allows one shot to be revised without destabilizing the full sequence.
- Gives us a persistent planning artifact we can improve over time.

---

## 2) Sequence Goal

Create a short sequence in which an **Acrocanthosaurus stalks through dense undergrowth, is clearly hunting, pushes through a boundary of bushes and cycads, and finally sees a group of sauropods in the distance**.

The scene should feel tense and deliberate rather than explosive. This is a **predator stalking reveal**, not yet an attack scene.

---

## 3) Global Continuity Rules

These should remain consistent across all shots unless deliberately changed.

### Subject
- **Main animal:** Acrocanthosaurus
- Large carcharodontosaur theropod
- Behavior: stealthy, focused, deliberate, predatory
- No roaring, no dramatic threat display, no sprinting

### Prey
- **Prey:** a small herd / group of sauropods
- Prey should only appear in the final reveal shot
- Sauropods should appear at a distance, not close-range

### Environment
- Early Cretaceous Texas-like environment
- Dense undergrowth with ferns, low brush, and cycads
- Transition into a more open clearing / floodplain / open woodland edge

### Lighting
- Natural daylight
- Keep sunlight direction consistent across shots
- Prefer soft warm daylight from camera-left or left-front and maintain consistently

### Screen Direction
- Acro should generally move **screen-right** throughout the stalking sequence
- Maintain coherent geography from shot to shot

### Mood
- Quiet tension
- Hunting intent is shown through posture, stillness, and controlled movement

### Negative Constraints
Avoid:
- roaring
- exaggerated cinematic monster behavior
- random direction changes
- sudden charging before the prey reveal
- overly sparse vegetation in the first half of the sequence

## 5) Detailed Scene Directions

---

## Scene 1 — Establish the Stalk

**Duration:** 2 sec  
**Purpose:** Immediately establish Acro as a stealthy predator moving through cover.

### Visual Concept
A medium side-view or three-quarter side-view of Acro moving slowly through dense brush. The body is partly obscured by ferns, brush, and low cycads. The posture is low and tense.

### Camera
- Medium shot
- Side or slight three-quarter side angle
- Stable or very subtle tracking motion

### Start Frame Direction (GPT Image)
**Prompt:**  
Create a cinematic Early Cretaceous scene showing an **Acrocanthosaurus stalking through dense undergrowth**. The animal is partially obscured by ferns, low bushes, and cycads. Show a medium side or three-quarter side view. The Acro is moving **screen-right**, with a stealthy predatory posture: head low, neck extended slightly forward, body tense, one foot lifted mid-step. Natural daylight, consistent warm sunlight from the left side. Dense prehistoric vegetation fills the foreground and midground. The mood is tense and quiet. No roaring, no open-mouth threat display.

### End Frame Direction (GPT Image)
**Prompt:**  
Create the end frame for the same scene. The **Acrocanthosaurus has advanced a few careful steps forward through the vegetation**, still moving screen-right. Keep the same environment and lighting. The foliage lightly brushes its flanks and lower body. Its neck is slightly more extended, and the hunting intent should be clear through a focused forward gaze. Maintain the same dense ferns, brush, and cycads. No prey visible yet.

### Kling Motion Prompt
Slow stealthy forward movement through undergrowth. Heavy but careful steps, subtle shoulder and torso motion, head low and focused. Vegetation brushes and shifts lightly as the Acro advances. Maintain tension and quiet hunting behavior.

### Continuity Notes
- Maintain strong vegetation density.
- Keep Acro moving rightward.
- No prey visible.

---

### Scene 1 Revision Note — Positional Delta
The first generated start/end pair was too similar. Future end frames must show Acro materially farther screen-right, with roughly 15–25% frame-width advancement and at least one clearly completed step. Vegetation changes alone are insufficient.

## Scene 2 — Hunting Cue Close-Up

**Duration:** 2 sec  
**Purpose:** Make it unmistakable that the animal is tracking prey.

### Visual Concept
A closer shot focusing on the head, neck, and upper torso framed by layered greenery. The Acro pauses and studies something off-screen ahead.

### Camera
- Medium close-up
- Slight three-quarter front or side-front angle
- Minimal camera movement

### Start Frame Direction (GPT Image)
**Prompt:**  
Create a closer cinematic shot of an **Acrocanthosaurus partially concealed behind prehistoric foliage**. Show the head, neck, and upper torso through layers of ferns and cycads. The Acro has just paused during a stalk, with its body mostly still. Its posture should show intense predatory focus. Lighting and environment should match the previous shot, with soft warm daylight from the left. No prey visible.

### End Frame Direction (GPT Image)
**Prompt:**  
Create the end frame for this close hunting shot. The **Acrocanthosaurus has lifted its head slightly and turned it just enough to lock its gaze toward something ahead off-screen**. Its expression and posture should clearly suggest prey detection. Keep the same foliage framing, same lighting, and same realistic predatory behavior. No roaring or dramatic display.

### Kling Motion Prompt
The Acro comes to a still pause, subtly raises its head, and makes a small deliberate head turn as if scenting or detecting prey. Emphasize stillness, intent focus, and restrained motion.

### Continuity Notes
- This scene should read as “I see or sense something.”
- Motion should be minimal and controlled.

---

## Scene 3 — Push Through Deeper Brush

**Duration:** 2 sec  
**Purpose:** Show the predator committing to a careful forward approach.

### Visual Concept
A lower-angle view shows Acro advancing into thicker brush. Plants partially block the body, emphasizing concealment and mass.

### Camera
- Low angle
- Front-quarter or side-front view
- Slight forward energy while maintaining stealth

### Start Frame Direction (GPT Image)
**Prompt:**  
Create a cinematic low-angle shot of an **Acrocanthosaurus approaching a denser wall of prehistoric vegetation**. Show it from a front-quarter or side-front angle while it continues moving screen-right. Dense cycads, bushes, and fern fronds fill the foreground and partially obscure the animal. The Acro is focused and hunting, with no roaring. Lighting should remain consistent with earlier shots.

### End Frame Direction (GPT Image)
**Prompt:**  
Create the end frame for the same shot. The **Acrocanthosaurus is now actively pushing into the denser vegetation**, with leaves and branches parting around its snout, chest, and shoulders. Keep the motion readable but controlled. The environment remains dense and prehistoric. No prey visible yet.

### Kling Motion Prompt
The Acro takes a few slow controlled steps into thicker brush. Its head and shoulders push vegetation aside. Leaves bend, fronds sway, and branches part slightly. Motion is powerful but restrained.

### Continuity Notes
- The animal should feel large and heavy.
- Vegetation interaction is important here.

---

## Scene 4 — Break the Vegetation Boundary

**Duration:** 2 sec  
**Purpose:** Transition from concealment to a viewing gap where the prey can be revealed.

### Visual Concept
The Acro pushes through a boundary of bushes and cycads toward a brighter, more open area.

### Camera
- Slightly behind or side-behind angle can work well
- Could also use a side angle if the plant boundary reads clearly

### Start Frame Direction (GPT Image)
**Prompt:**  
Create a cinematic scene showing a dense **vegetation boundary of bushes, cycads, and prehistoric undergrowth**, with an Acrocanthosaurus just behind it, about to emerge. The view should suggest that a more open area lies beyond the foliage. Lighting should remain consistent, with daylight filtering through leaves. The Acro is still in hunting mode and moving screen-right.

### End Frame Direction (GPT Image)
**Prompt:**  
Create the end frame for this transition shot. The **Acrocanthosaurus has pushed through the vegetation boundary enough that its head, neck, and upper torso are now visible past the foliage**, and a more open landscape can be seen beyond. The animal is focused and alert, not roaring. Keep the same daylight and realistic predator behavior.

### Kling Motion Prompt
The Acro pushes through a dense barrier of cycads and bushes. Leaves and branches part clearly as the animal emerges into a better view position. The motion should feel deliberate, not explosive.

### Continuity Notes
- This is the doorway into the reveal.
- Make the open space beyond readable.

---

## Scene 5 — Reveal the Sauropods

**Duration:** 4 sec  
**Purpose:** Deliver the visual payoff: Acro sees the prey in the distance.

### Visual Concept
Use a wide or over-the-shoulder composition. Acro occupies the foreground or midground while a group of sauropods is visible in the distance in an open landscape.

### Camera
- Over-the-shoulder or rear three-quarter view preferred
- Wide enough to clearly show prey in the distance
- Hold long enough for the reveal to register

### Start Frame Direction (GPT Image)
**Prompt:**  
Create a cinematic over-the-shoulder or wide rear three-quarter shot of an **Acrocanthosaurus emerging from dense prehistoric vegetation and looking out into a more open Early Cretaceous landscape**. The Acro should be in the foreground or midground, partly framed by plants. In the distance, reveal a **small group of sauropods** browsing or moving slowly across an open clearing, floodplain, or woodland edge. Maintain realistic scale and distance so the prey clearly feels far away. Consistent daylight and mood.

### End Frame Direction (GPT Image)
**Prompt:**  
Create the end frame for the prey reveal. The **Acrocanthosaurus has settled into a still hunting posture, fully focused on the distant sauropod group**. The sauropods remain in the distance and may be slowly moving or browsing. Keep the lighting and environment consistent. The shot should feel like a tense visual payoff rather than an attack.

### Kling Motion Prompt
The Acro completes its emergence from the brush and becomes still, subtly lifting or angling its head toward the distant sauropods. The prey animals move slowly in the far background. Let the reveal breathe and emphasize quiet tension.

### Continuity Notes
- This is the first time prey is visible.
- Do not turn this into an attack shot yet.

## 6) Production Notes

### Best Use of GPT Image
For each scene, generate:
- **one start frame**
- **one end frame**

These should be visually consistent and written so the second image feels like a plausible later moment in the same shot.

### Best Use of Kling Canvas
Use the canvas to:
- arrange the 5 scenes in order
- connect start and end frames to each scene
- keep reference characters/environments persistent
- help preserve continuity

### Best Use of Kling 3.0
Generate **each scene separately** instead of making one long multi-shot generation. This keeps control at the shot level and makes troubleshooting much easier.
